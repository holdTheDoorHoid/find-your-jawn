## What failed and why

**engage_drexel and engage_ccp (Campus Labs Engage, Drexel and CCP student orgs).** The task
brief named the exact endpoint to hit:
`https://<subdomain>.campuslabs.com/engage/api/discovery/search/organizations`. Both
`drexel.campuslabs.com/robots.txt` and `ccp.campuslabs.com/robots.txt` carry
`Disallow: /engage/api/` under `User-agent: *`, checked fresh today. CLAUDE.md is unconditional
here: never get around a robots rule. So the harvester checks robots.txt first and refuses,
rather than fetching anyway. The parsing code is written and unit tested against the saved
sample payloads, so it is ready the moment either school opens that path, or if the owner wants
to revisit this with the schools directly. This is the one real conflict between the task brief
and a binding rule; flagging it here rather than quietly working around it.

**nss_grottos, possible small gap.** The Pennsylvania grotto listing page shows "1 2 Next"
pagination, but a direct fetch of `state/pennsylvania/page/2/` returned the same single grotto
already on page 1, not new ones. This looks like an AJAX "load more" control that a plain HTTP
fetch cannot drive. We kept the 10 grottos visible in the static page rather than guess at what a
page 2 might hold. Philadelphia Grotto (Media) and Bucks County Grotto (Warrington) are flagged
`extra.philadelphia_area: true`.

**Two old volunteer portals, as the owner expected.** `serve.volunteermatch.org` (the 2022
Mayor's Volunteer Portal) does not resolve at all (DNS failure). `volunteer.phila.gov` times out.
Neither was harvested; both are recorded in `registry/sources.yaml` so nobody rediscovers them
by accident.

**Everything else ran clean.** No other source returned an error, a CAPTCHA, or a login wall.

## Surprises worth knowing

**The IRS Business Master File does not contain revoked organizations at all.** The scouting
report guessed that the STATUS field would let us filter out revoked nonprofits later. That
turned out to be wrong: IRS Publication 5926, the file's own dataset guide, states this extract
only ever carries status 01 (unconditional exemption), 02 (conditional exemption, no longer
issued), or 25 (an organization terminating its private foundation status). Revoked
organizations are excluded from the file entirely, by the IRS's own design. In our 9,664
Philadelphia records, 9,646 carry status 01, 13 carry status 12 (a trust category not even
documented in the current guide, likely a legacy code), and 5 carry status 25. None of that is a
revocation count. A real revoked/active split would need a different IRS file or the ProPublica
Nonprofit Explorer API, not this one.

**The 990-N filers skew old.** Of the 6,895 Philadelphia e-Postcard filers, only 3,257 (47
percent) last filed for tax year 2024 or 2025. The rest range all the way back to 2007; one
sample record found during testing had not filed since 2010. This is exactly the pattern
`docs/DATA_MODEL.md` already expects ("small groups often stop filing and keep meeting"), now
with real numbers behind it: IRS revocation, or simply not filing, is weak evidence of a group
being gone. 124 of the 6,895 are explicitly marked terminated by the filer. Only 2,491 (36
percent) list a website.

**Most RCO contact emails are personal, not organizational.** Of 240 current RCOs, all 240 carry
a named primary contact and a phone number, and 165 (69 percent) use a personal email provider
(Gmail, Yahoo, and similar) rather than an organization's own domain. The scouting report flagged
this as a possibility; now it is a measured fact, and whoever decides what the public site shows
for a "contact" should see this number before deciding.

**Ward committees are mixed into the RCO layer.** 26 of the 240 current RCO records carry
`org_type: Ward`, meaning Democratic or Republican ward committees that also registered as
Registered Community Organizations for zoning notice purposes. CLAUDE.md puts partisan political
groups out of scope for the public site. We did not filter them out of the raw lead file (leads
are supposed to be unfiltered, triage is a later step, same as the IRS files), but whoever does
that triage should know to drop these 26 before anything reaches the site.

**The Mummers lineup page structure changed since the scouting pass.** This year's page has 1
Fancy Division entry, 10 Wench Brigade entries, 32 Comic Division clubs under 3 mother clubs
(Landi Comics NYA: 4, Rich Porco's Murray Comic Club: 17, Goodtimers NYA: 11), and 14 String Band
entries, for 57 total, a different shape than the scouting report's count (which guessed roughly
10 fancy brigades). We trust the live page over the earlier guess.

**The Cultural Fund directory count matched the scouting report exactly: 322 grantees**, with
Multi-disciplinary (66), Music (50), Theatre (39), and Dance (38) the largest discipline tags.
Only 2 of 322 grantees have no website listed.

**The City's volunteer portal mixes programs and genuinely useful opportunity detail, with one
real gap.** The 39 opportunity pages (`kind_hint: opportunity`) carry age and other requirements,
a location, and a link back to the parent program, cleanly. The "Begins / Registration Closes /
Duration / Open Spots" shift table is server rendered for some opportunities (24 of 39 carry real
shift rows) and left for JavaScript to fill in for the rest; we record whatever is present rather
than guess, so `extra.shifts` is an empty list for 15 of the 39.

**phila.gov has no real department directory.** `https://www.phila.gov/departments/` turned out
to be a landing page of broad service categories and elected officials, not a list of
individual department homepages, despite its name. A generic crawl from it alone would have
found almost nothing. The harvester instead leans on the specific known pages the owner gave,
plus one hop of keyword matched links from those pages. The approach worked: 23 distinct City
program pages and 18 external partner organizations (Love Your Park, Fairmount Park Conservancy,
TreePhilly, the Salvation Army, Pennsylvania Animal Response Team, SEPA VOAD, AmeriCorps Seniors,
Broad Street Run, and others) came out of it. A first pass also pulled in a lot of site-wide
footer noise (SEPTA, Visit Philadelphia, City Council, the District Attorney, a 311 widget, a
browser upgrade nag) from the `/departments/` landing page itself; those domains are now
excluded. `serve.phila.gov` was checked directly and, as expected, just redirects to the Office
of Community Empowerment and Opportunity's department page rather than being its own program.
